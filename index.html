Redispatch: model vs what REE really did

How much the grid stage turned plants up and down, next to REE's own redispatch for grid security (e·sios). REE acts on the day-ahead programme and starts plants, mostly gas, where the grid needs them; the model acts on its balancing schedule.

Extra cost of redispatch

Why the difference?

Pooled to match the model's single redispatch stage: REE's technical constraints after the day-ahead market (phase I turns plants up or down for grid security, phase II rebalances) plus real-time constraints, Spanish peninsula, from e·sios (volumes 701–704, 720, 721; costs 1373–1376, 1723, 1724). REE pays what goes up its own bid including start-up costs, and refunds what goes down at its bid or the day-ahead price (P.O. 3.2, P.O. 14.4); the cost is the extra cost over the market, paid by consumers. Model: AC redispatch minus balancing over all units except the border flows, valued at the model's marginal costs; it includes grid losses, and failed AC hours have no value.

Balancing: the model's BAL stage vs REE's balancing markets

The model's balancing price next to REE's balancing energy prices, and how much balancing energy REE used. Spain has no single balancing price: RR (replacement reserve) is the hourly market closest to the model's BAL stage; aFRR is the automatic reserve.

Largest plants

Real vs model uses ENTSO-E's measured output per unit, matched to the model's plants in entsoe_unit_crosswalk.csv; plants ENTSO-E reports only in part are left out. Output and change are from the AC redispatch; "Redispatch change" is redispatch output minus balancing output, so it shows what the grid stage changed. Hydro includes pumped storage (pumping is not counted as output). Not shown: the model's industrial CHP, waste and mini-hydro blocks (spread over 100 substations, so not at real locations) and load shedding. Failed AC hours are left out of averages.
